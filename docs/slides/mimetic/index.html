MIMETIC

Open generative models for sound, music and video, trained only on data we have the right to use.

Creative Computing Institute, University of the Arts London · Durham University
Framing

We don't compete on scale

The large labs keep ownership. We hand it to the user.

Data

Every source documented

So far: our generators, pre-1963 UK recordings and Freesound CC0 (Creative Commons Zero). Anything added must be ours to use.

Generators

One recipe, thousands of variations

Each generator is a recipe with adjustable settings, such as tempo or brightness. We work through many combinations, so the model learns the full range rather than a few examples.

Method

One method for sound and video

A frozen VAE (variational autoencoder), a small DiT (diffusion transformer), then fine-tuning.

Order

Audio first, then video

Audio on our Blackwell cards. Video on Durham's Grace Hopper nodes.

First result

cc0-sampler

A 326M-parameter DiT on clean audio. 200k steps in about 30 hours.

Measure

Can it be fine-tuned cleanly?

A narrow fine-tune should approach what the VAE can reconstruct.

Release

Open, and built for ordinary hardware

Anyone who owns their material can fine-tune privately. Base models not trained on real faces or voices.
